Overview of MixAgent

Walkthrough of one mix

A jazz clip with 15 input tracks, followed through all three levels. Qwen plan only keeps Qwen's initial settings at every level, and its subgroup and full-mix examples use the uncorrected track outputs. MixAgent applies corrections at each level.

Click a level in the diagram, or use the tabs below, to hear that level.

Listen to the examples

🎚️What each player is

  • Style reference: the sound to aim for, from another part of the same song.
  • Professional mix: the engineer's mix of these exact 15 seconds. No system hears it.
  • MixAgent: our system, with a trained detector and critic making numerical corrections.
  • Gemini 3.8 Flash, GPT-4o-audio, Qwen3-Omni: general audio-language models given the same tracks, reference and effects library, each writing its plan in one pass.
  • MST, Diff-MST, StemFX: prior neural mixing systems that match a reference's style in one pass. MST and StemFX are 4-stem based.
  • Different-song reference (listening test): the style reference comes from a different song, so there is no exact target. There, the professional mix is the song's own mix, shown as an anchor. Only MST was rated by listeners. Diff-MST and StemFX are shown for comparison.

🎧What to listen for

  1. ⚖️Balance: can you hear every part, with the lead voice or instrument on top and nothing buried?
  2. 🎛️Tone: is it clear, or muddy (too much low-mid), thin (too little bass) or harsh (too much treble)?
  3. 🥁Dynamics: does it breathe and punch, or does it sound squashed or jumpy?
  4. ↔️Space: is it spread across left and right with sensible reverb, or collapsed to the middle?
  5. 🎯Style: how close does it get to the style reference?

📊Reading the small charts under each player

Each bar shows how much more (up) or less (down) energy a mix has in one frequency band than the target, in dB. Low is bass and kick (below 200 Hz), Lo-mid is the body of most instruments (200 Hz to 1 kHz), Hi-mid is presence and bite (1 to 5 kHz), and High is cymbals and air (above 5 kHz).

Grey bars are within 3 dB of the target. Orange means too much, for example harsh or boomy. Blue means too little, for example thin or dull. The grey line at the bottom is stereo width, and the amber tick marks the target's width. This example, Gemini on the opera clip, is thin in the bass and almost mono.
